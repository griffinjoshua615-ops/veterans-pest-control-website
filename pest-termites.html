Signs to look for

How to tell if you have termites

  • Mud tubes about the width of a pencil running up foundation walls, piers or crawlspace surfaces
  • Wood that sounds hollow when tapped, or gives under light pressure from a screwdriver
  • Floors that feel spongy, or trim and baseboards that flex
  • Winged swarmers indoors, especially in spring, and discarded wings piled on window sills
  • Bubbling or uneven paint that looks like water damage with no water source
  • Doors and windows that suddenly stick as frames distort

Why it matters here

What termites mean for an Alabama property

Alabama is prime termite country

Warm, humid conditions and a long season mean subterranean termites stay active here far longer each year than they do further north. Eastern subterranean termites are found throughout the state. The more aggressive Formosan subterranean termite has become established in Alabama as well, concentrated around the Gulf Coast and spreading inland.

They work where you cannot see

Subterranean termites live in the soil and reach wood through mud tubes, foundation cracks, plumbing penetrations and anywhere wood contacts the ground. Damage happens inside studs, joists and sills, which is why it stays hidden until something feels soft or sounds hollow.

Homeowners insurance usually will not cover it

Termite damage is typically treated as preventable maintenance rather than a sudden event, so the repair bill tends to land on the owner. Prevention and early detection are the cheap part of this problem.

Before we get there

What you can do yourself

None of this replaces treatment, but it reduces the pressure and makes the work hold longer.

  • Keep mulch, soil and firewood pulled back from the foundation, and keep siding clear of the ground
  • Fix leaking spigots, gutters and downspouts, and direct water away from the foundation
  • Keep crawlspace vents open and unobstructed
  • Do not store lumber, cardboard or firewood against the house or under the crawlspace
  • If you see swarmers, collect a few in a bag rather than sweeping them all up, because identification matters

When to act

Spring is when swarmers appear and most people first call, but subterranean termites feed year round in Alabama, so the colony does not wait for a season. If you are buying or selling, start the inspection well before the closing date rather than the week of it.

Questions

Termites questions we get asked

Are flying ants and termite swarmers the same thing?

No, and telling them apart matters. Termite swarmers have a straight waist, two pairs of wings the same length, and straight antennae. Flying ants have a pinched waist, front wings longer than back wings, and bent antennae. If you are not sure, save a couple in a sealed bag and we will identify them.

I had a termite treatment years ago. Am I still protected?

Not necessarily. Treatments have a service life, soil gets disturbed by landscaping and plumbing work, and a lapsed agreement means nobody has looked in a long time. If it has been years since anyone inspected, get it inspected.

Do I need an inspection to sell my house?

Lenders and buyers commonly require a wood-destroying insect report as part of an Alabama closing, and there is usually a deadline attached. Tell us the closing date when you call. Confirm the exact report your lender requires, since it varies by loan type.
