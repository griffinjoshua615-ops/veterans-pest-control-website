Spiders

Spider Control in Selma & Central Alabama

Most spiders in an Alabama home are harmless and are actually eating other pests. Two are worth taking seriously, and a spider problem is usually a signal about the insects they are feeding on.

Signs to look for

How to tell if you have spiders

  • Webs in corners, eaves, garage ceilings, crawlspace entries and around exterior lights
  • Funnel or sheet webs in ground cover and along foundations
  • Egg sacs, pale and papery, tucked into sheltered corners
  • Tangled, irregular webs low down in sheltered outdoor spots, which is typical of widows
  • Spiders repeatedly found in stored boxes, shoes and seldom-used closets

Why it matters here

What spiders mean for an Alabama property

Two species here warrant caution

Brown recluse and black widow both occur in Alabama. Recluse spiders favor undisturbed places such as boxes, closets, attics and behind stored items. Widows favor sheltered outdoor spots like meter boxes, woodpiles, crawlspace entries and under outdoor furniture. Bites are uncommon but worth avoiding.

Spiders follow their food

A steady spider population usually means a steady insect population. Exterior lighting that pulls insects to the house is one of the most common drivers, and reducing the prey does more for the spider count than spraying webs.

Webs tell you where to work

Web placement shows travel routes and harborage. Removing webs is part of the work, not because it kills anything, but because it resets the picture and tells you what is still active.

Before we get there

What you can do yourself

None of this replaces treatment, but it reduces the pressure and makes the work hold longer.

  • Store items in sealed plastic bins rather than cardboard, especially in garages, attics and closets
  • Shake out shoes, gloves and stored clothing that has sat undisturbed
  • Switch exterior lights to warm or yellow bulbs, which draw far fewer insects and therefore fewer spiders
  • Keep firewood off the ground and away from the house, and wear gloves when moving it
  • Clear webs regularly from eaves, corners and meter boxes

Questions

Spiders questions we get asked

How do I know if it is a brown recluse?

The reliable marker is six eyes in three pairs rather than the usual eight, and a uniformly colored leg with no banding or spines. The violin marking gets over-relied on and is often misread. If you are unsure, catch it in a sealed container and let us look.

I was bitten. What should I do?

Seek medical advice rather than waiting to see how it develops, especially for a suspected recluse or widow bite, and bring the spider if you safely can. That is a doctor's call, not a pest control call.

Do I need treatment if the spiders are harmless?

Not always. If the count is low and they are outdoor species, they are doing you a favor. It becomes worth treating when they are indoors in numbers, in storage areas, or when the species is one of the two worth avoiding.
