Signs to look for

How to tell if you have cockroaches

  • Egg cases, small, dark and capsule shaped, glued into cracks and behind appliances
  • Droppings that look like coarse pepper or, from larger roaches, small dark pellets
  • A musty, oily smell that gets stronger as numbers grow
  • Roaches visible in daylight, which usually means the harborage is already crowded
  • Smear marks along baseboards and under sinks in damp areas

Why it matters here

What cockroaches mean for an Alabama property

German cockroaches live and breed indoors

They are small, tan, with two dark stripes behind the head, and they stay near warmth and moisture: kitchens, behind appliances, around dishwashers and under sinks. One egg case carries dozens of eggs, and they are indoors permanently, so the population is self-sustaining until it is dealt with properly.

Large roaches are usually coming in from outside

American and smokybrown cockroaches breed in mulch, sewers, tree holes and crawlspaces, then come in through gaps, drains and doors. For these, sealing entry points and fixing moisture does more than repeat spraying indoors.

They are a health and inspection problem, not just a nuisance

Roach allergens are a recognized asthma trigger, and for a restaurant or food business a roach sighting is a business problem the same day it happens.

Before we get there

What you can do yourself

None of this replaces treatment, but it reduces the pressure and makes the work hold longer.

  • Fix leaks under sinks and around dishwashers, since moisture is the limiting factor for most roach populations
  • Store food, including pet food, in sealed containers and empty trash nightly
  • Seal gaps around plumbing penetrations, door sweeps and utility entries
  • Pull mulch, leaf litter and firewood back from the foundation for the outdoor species
  • Avoid fogging or bug bombs, which push roaches deeper into walls and spread them between units

Questions

Cockroaches questions we get asked

Why do roaches come back after I spray?

Usually because the spray reached the ones walking around and not the harborage where the rest live and breed, or because the species is coming in from outside and the entry points are still open.

I only see them at night. Is it bad?

Not necessarily, that is normal roach behavior. Seeing them during the day is the bigger warning sign, because it often means the hiding places are crowded.

Does one roach mean an infestation?

For a large outdoor species, one can genuinely be one that wandered in. For German cockroaches, one usually means more, because they do not travel alone and they do not live outdoors here.
